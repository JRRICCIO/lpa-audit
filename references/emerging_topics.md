# Emerging fund-formation topics

Beyond the 18 standard review areas, the following topics have become market-relevant since 2022 and increasingly appear in modern Luxembourg SCSp / ASLP LPAs. Flag if present, flag the silence if material.

## E1. Subscription lines and NAV facilities

Market context: subscription credit facilities (secured by LP capital commitments) and NAV facilities (secured by the fund's portfolio NAV) have become common. They smooth capital calls, manage timing, and can boost IRR. They also create leverage and timing distortions.

Check:
- Authorisation for subscription lines: cap, tenor (typically max 12 months per ILPA), permitted uses.
- Authorisation for NAV facilities: cap, tenor, permitted uses (NAV facilities are increasingly controversial when used for distributions, not just investments).
- Disclosure obligations: LPA should require regular reporting of facility usage to LPs.
- Interaction with leverage caps (AIFMD II): subscription lines should count or not count toward the 175%/300% cap, per the LPA's definition of leverage.
- LPAC consent for NAV facilities above a threshold or for distributions funded by NAV facilities.

Red flags:
- Unlimited subscription line authority with no cap or tenor (AMBER).
- NAV facility authority with no LPAC consent threshold (AMBER to RED if material).
- NAV facility used to fund distributions with no disclosure to LPs (RED, ILPA flagged this as concerning in 2024 guidance).

## E2. Continuation funds and GP-led secondaries

Market context: GP-led secondary transactions, including continuation fund vehicles, have grown rapidly. They create acute conflicts: the GP is on both sides (selling from the existing fund, buying into the continuation vehicle).

Check:
- Does the LPA address GP-led secondaries explicitly?
- LPAC role: ILPA recommends LPAC approval of continuation fund transactions as a precondition.
- LP options: ILPA recommends LPs have status-quo (no action required), sell, or roll options with sufficient information and time.
- Independent valuation requirement for the secondary transaction.

Red flags:
- LPA silent on GP-led secondaries (AMBER).
- GP empowered to execute GP-led secondaries without LPAC consent (RED, ILPA-non-conforming).
- LPs not given a status-quo option in the LPA's secondaries framework (RED).

## E3. Co-investment rights

Market context: co-investments are now a major LP demand. The LPA should set the framework, not just leave it to side letters.

Check:
- Allocation methodology: pro-rata to commitments, first-come-first-served, GP discretion, tiered by commitment size.
- Fees and carry on co-investments: typically no-fee no-carry (ILPA-aligned) or reduced.
- Co-investment costs: who pays for broken deals, due diligence, legal expenses.
- Most-favoured-nation language for co-investment rights.

Red flags:
- Co-investment allocation entirely at GP discretion (AMBER, but very common).
- Co-investors charged full management fee and carry on co-investment positions (AMBER to RED depending on size).
- Broken deal expenses borne disproportionately by LPs vs co-investors (AMBER).

## E4. ESG and SFDR depth

Market context: SFDR has matured. Article 8 / Article 9 classification claims must now be backed by operational machinery in the LPA.

Check:
- Article 8 funds: pre-contractual disclosure (Annex II template), periodic reporting on E/S characteristics, ESG diligence machinery embedded in investment process.
- Article 9 funds: pre-contractual disclosure (Annex III template), sustainable objective with measurable indicators, DNSH assessment, principal adverse impacts (PAI) consideration.
- Divestment triggers for sustainability breaches.
- ESG data obligations from portfolio companies.

Red flags:
- LPA claims Article 9 but investment policy reads as general PE / VC with no measurable sustainable objective (RED, mis-classification risk).
- LPA claims Article 8 with no ESG diligence procedure or KPI reporting (AMBER to RED).
- Portfolio company ESG data obligations absent for an Article 8 / Article 9 fund (AMBER).

## E5. ELTIF 2.0 alignment (if applicable)

Market context: ELTIF 2.0 (Regulation (EU) 2023/606, in force January 2024) allows retail investors to access EU private market funds with a passport. ELTIF 2.0 LPAs have additional constraints.

Check:
- Retail eligibility: eligible asset rules (70% in long-term assets), diversification, leverage caps.
- Liquidity: redemption window options, minimum 5-year initial holding for retail.
- Master-feeder structures: ELTIF master with non-retail feeders is now common.
- Marketing passport: pre-marketing rules, distribution wrapper alignment.

This is OUT OF SCOPE for the standard SCSp / ASLP audit unless the LPA expressly identifies the vehicle as an ELTIF 2.0. If flagged, recommend a separate ELTIF-specific review.

## E6. Tax allocation and partnership representative

Market context: SCSp is tax-transparent at vehicle level, but the LPA must allocate income, gain, and loss among partners. US LPs in particular need clean allocation language to support their tax filings.

Check:
- Allocation methodology: typically follows distributions (target capital account allocations) for US LPs.
- Partnership representative or equivalent (for US-tax-exempt LPs).
- Withholding and tax payment mechanics (foreign withholding, FATCA, CRS, DAC6).
- Tax distributions: minimum distributions to cover LP tax liability on phantom income.

Red flags:
- Tax allocation language inconsistent with US partnership tax conventions for LPs that need them (AMBER, requires US tax counsel verification).
- No tax distribution provision in a fund with US LPs and phantom income exposure (AMBER).

## E7. Confidentiality and public disclosure

Market context: large public LPs (state pensions, sovereign wealth funds) often have freedom-of-information obligations. The LPA must accommodate them without compromising fund confidentiality.

Check:
- Confidentiality regime stated.
- Public-disclosure carveouts for LPs subject to FOI / sunshine laws.
- LP information about other LPs (the quarterly LP list) - confidentiality of LP identity.
- GP press release / case study consent.

Red flags:
- No confidentiality regime (RED for a fund holding sensitive portfolio company data).
- No carveout for FOI-obligated LPs (AMBER).
- LP names shareable in marketing without consent (AMBER).
