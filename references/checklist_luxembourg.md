# Luxembourg SCSp / ASLP checklist (areas 16 to 18)

Authority: Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies (as amended, including the 2013 reform that introduced the SCSp); Law of 12 July 2013 on AIFMs (transposing AIFMD I and AIFMD II); CSSF Circular 25/901 (19 December 2025); CSSF Circular 18/698 on substance.

The SCSp (Société en Commandite Spéciale, marketed internationally as ASLP or Alternative Special Limited Partnership) is governed by Articles 22-1 to 22-11 of the 1915 Law. It has no legal personality, no statutory minimum capital, and almost complete contractual freedom in the LPA.

## 16. SCSp Law of 2013 compliance

Mandatory elements:
- At least one general partner (associé commandité) with unlimited, joint and several liability.
- At least one limited partner (associé commanditaire) with liability capped at commitment.
- Registered office in Luxembourg.
- Manager (gérant), which is often the GP but can be a third party.
- Registration in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS).
- An LPA (which is the operating document; the SCSp has no statutes in the corporate sense).

Confidentiality regime:
- The LP register is kept internally by the GP/manager. Only the GP, the registered office, and the manager are publicly registered.
- The LPA itself is private and not filed in full with the RCS.
- This is a major commercial advantage versus SCS or SCA structures.

Tax transparency:
- The SCSp is tax-transparent at the vehicle level. Income flows to partners with their own tax profile.
- The LPA may state this for clarity but the tax treatment derives from law, not contract.

Check:
- GP named with full legal name, RCS number, registered office.
- LP commitments structured as commitments to a "Fund" (the SCSp) with capital calls.
- Manager named (typically the GP or an affiliated SARL).
- AIFM appointment, if the SCSp is an AIF above thresholds.
- Confidentiality of LP register affirmed (it is the law, but worth confirming).
- No accidental statutes-based language that would only apply to SCS or SCA (the SCSp is fully contractual).

Red flags:
- LPA references "statutory rights of limited partners" without grounding (AMBER, sloppy drafting).
- LP commitments not in writing or unsigned at first close (RED, structural problem).
- Manager and GP confused or mis-named (RED).

## 17. Information rights

Standard:
- Quarterly capital account statements, within 45 to 60 days of quarter end.
- Audited annual financial statements, within 90 to 120 days of year end.
- Annual AIFM report under Annex IV (the LP-facing version).
- Ad-hoc reporting on material events: key person triggers, major investments, defaults, regulatory inquiries.
- LPAC reporting cadence (typically at least annually, often quarterly).
- Tax reporting: Schedule K-1 equivalents (for US LPs), CRS / FATCA outputs.

Check:
- Quarterly cadence stated for capital accounts.
- Annual audit firm named (or LPAC approves).
- Reporting language (English typically for international LPs).
- Format (PDF + Excel or similar).
- Penalties or LP remedies for late reporting.

Red flags:
- Quarterly capital accounts not promised (AMBER).
- Audit firm "to be appointed by the GP" with no LPAC consent (AMBER, ILPA-non-conforming).
- LP information rights subject to a confidentiality clause that the GP can invoke broadly (AMBER).
- No reporting language stated (AMBER, sloppy).

## 18. Default, transfer, and recycling provisions

### LP default

Standard mechanics:
- Notice of default after missed capital call.
- Cure period (typically 10 to 30 days).
- Default penalty: forfeiture of all or a portion of the commitment, sale to other LPs, interest on the missed call, suspension of voting rights.

Check:
- Default trigger objectively defined.
- Cure period stated.
- Penalty mechanic specified (typically forfeiture of 50% to 100% of contributed amount, plus interest).
- Forced sale to other LPs or to a third party authorised by the GP.

Red flags:
- Default penalty entirely at the GP's discretion (AMBER, LP-unfavorable).
- 100% forfeiture as the only remedy (AMBER, too harsh).
- No cure period (RED, unenforceable in Luxembourg in some cases).

### Transfer

Standard mechanics:
- Transfer requires GP consent.
- Permitted transfers (affiliates, estate planning vehicles, ERISA-driven transfers) without consent.
- Right of first refusal or right of first offer in favour of other LPs (optional, ILPA-supportive).

Check:
- Transfer regime stated.
- Permitted transfers defined.
- GP consent standard ("reasonable consent" vs "in its sole discretion").
- Transferee onboarding (AML, KYC, regulatory eligibility).

Red flags:
- Transfer requires GP consent in its sole discretion with no carveouts (AMBER).
- No mechanism for affiliate transfers (AMBER, friction for LPs).

### Recycling and follow-ons

Standard mechanics:
- Recycling: distributions returned to the fund and re-invested.
- Cap: typically 100% of commitments, sometimes 110% to 120%.
- Time window: typically only within the investment period.
- Source: which distributions can be recycled (return of capital vs realised gains).

Check:
- Recycling permitted? Cap? Window?
- Source of recyclable distributions specified.
- Follow-on investments: are they part of the commitment, or unlimited?

Red flags:
- Recycling permitted up to 200% with no time limit (AMBER, LP-unfavorable, commits LPs to potentially unlimited drawdowns).
- Follow-on authority unlimited and uncapped (AMBER).
