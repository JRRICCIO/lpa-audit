# ILPA Principles 3.0 checklist (areas 8 to 15)

Authority: ILPA Principles 3.0 (2019), the ILPA Model LPA (Whole-of-Fund version, 2019, revised 2020) and the Deal-by-Deal Model LPA (2020). Subsequent ILPA guidance on continuation funds, NAV facilities, subscription lines, and fee reporting.

ILPA is not law. It is the dominant market reference for LP-favorable terms. A deviation from ILPA is not a regulatory finding, it is a market-positioning finding. Flag accordingly: most deviations are AMBER, not RED.

## 8. Distribution waterfall

Two industry models:

### Whole-of-fund waterfall (ILPA-preferred)
LPs receive their full capital contributions and a preferred return BEFORE the GP receives any carried interest. Then a catch-up brings the GP up to its target percentage of profits, after which profits are split per the carried interest split.

### Deal-by-deal waterfall (American-style)
Carried interest is paid on each profitable exit, with clawback at fund termination if LPs were short.

Check:
- Which model does the LPA use?
- Internal consistency: are the hurdle rate, catch-up percentage, and carried interest split all defined and arithmetically consistent?
- Hurdle rate definition: typically 8% IRR, compounding annually. Check for tricks (simple interest, no compounding, return-based vs IRR-based).
- Catch-up: typically 50% to 100% (GP catches up to 20% of total profits). Check whether the catch-up is fully GP-favorable or 50/50.
- Carried interest split: typically 20/80 (80 to LPs, 20 to GP). European/Asian funds sometimes 15/85 or 17.5/82.5.
- Clawback: must be present in deal-by-deal waterfalls. Must be supported by escrow or guarantee.

Red flags:
- Hurdle rate defined as "simple interest" with no compounding (AMBER, LP-unfavorable).
- Deal-by-deal waterfall with no clawback (RED, ILPA-non-conforming, exposes LPs).
- Clawback obligation with no escrow, no guarantee, no joint-and-several liability among carry recipients (AMBER, weak enforcement).
- Catch-up faster than 100% (GP receives more than 20% during catch-up phase) (AMBER).

Additional ILPA-aligned details:
- Preferred return must accrue until ALL contributed capital is returned and unpaid preferred return is paid to LPs. If accrual stops earlier: AMBER to RED.
- Clawback escrow should be at least 30% of carried interest distributions (ILPA Principles 3.0 standard). If lower or absent: AMBER.
- Personal guarantees from ultimate carried interest recipients (potentially joint and several) should back the clawback. If absent: AMBER, especially for first-time managers or thinly capitalised GPs.
- LP giveback (LPs return distributions to fund indemnity obligations) and GP giveback (GP returns distributions in certain circumstances) are both expected. If only one direction: AMBER.
- Carried interest haircut for termination of the Fund should apply on the same basis as for-cause and without-cause removal. If termination is excluded: AMBER.
- Valuation methodology must be specifically identified in the LPA (not just "as determined by the GP"). If silent or generic: AMBER.

## 9. Management fee

Industry norms:
- Rate: typically 1.5% to 2.5% for buyout/growth; can be higher for venture; lower for private credit and real estate.
- Basis: committed capital during investment period, then invested or net invested capital during harvest period.
- Step-downs: ILPA recommends step-down at end of investment period.
- Fee offsets: transaction fees, monitoring fees, directors' fees, advisory fees from portfolio companies should offset management fee, ideally at 100%.
- Fee waiver during extensions: ILPA Model LPA specifies a full waiver of fees during fund term extensions.

Check:
- Rate stated, basis stated.
- Step-down at end of investment period: present, amount, trigger.
- Fee offsets: what % is offset, what fees count, are these stated mechanically or "as the GP determines"?
- Extension fees: are they full rate, reduced, or waived?

Red flags:
- Management fee on committed capital with no step-down at end of investment period (AMBER, LP-unfavorable).
- Fee offsets less than 100% with no clear rationale (AMBER).
- Management fee continues at full rate during extensions (AMBER, AMBER to RED depending on duration of extension period).
- Step-down formula does not account for realisations (so management fee stays high even as portfolio is sold down) (AMBER, ILPA July 2020 update specifically clarifies this).

## 10. GP commitment

Industry norms:
- Typically 1% to 5% of total fund commitments.
- Should be cash, not fee waivers or borrowed funds, for credibility.
- Often funded pari passu with LPs (drawn down on same capital call schedule).

Check:
- Amount specified.
- Form specified (cash vs fee waiver).
- Funding mechanics specified.
- Restrictions on the GP transferring or financing its commitment.

Red flags:
- GP commitment "in such amount as the GP determines" (RED, no skin in the game).
- GP commitment funded by fee waiver only (AMBER, weak alignment of interest).
- GP allowed to finance its commitment via a related-party loan (AMBER).

## 11. Key person provisions

Industry standard:
- Definition of "key person" (typically named individuals).
- Trigger events (death, disability, departure, dedication-of-time).
- Automatic suspension of investment period on trigger.
- Cure period (typically 90 to 180 days) to replace the key person.
- LPAC or LP approval of replacement.
- If not cured: termination of investment period, or LP-vote to remove GP.

Check:
- Are key persons named (not just "the senior partners")?
- Definition of trigger includes the "dedication of time" prong (failure to dedicate substantially all business time to the fund)?
- Automatic suspension on trigger?
- Cure period stated?
- LPAC or supermajority LP role in cure / replacement?

Red flags:
- Key person provision exists but trigger is "death only" with no dedication-of-time test (AMBER).
- No automatic suspension on trigger, only on LPAC vote (AMBER, slow).
- Cure period longer than 180 days (AMBER).
- No mechanism if cure fails (RED).
- No automatic termination of the investment period if LPs fail to approve a remediation plan (AMBER, ILPA-recommended).

## 12. LPAC composition and powers

ILPA-recommended powers:
- Approve the auditor at inception and any change.
- Consent to extensions of the investment period or the fund term.
- Approve all affiliate transactions (even if at arm's length, to ensure informed consent).
- Approve valuation methodology and approve material deviations from it.
- Approve fund reserves at wind-up.
- Approve cross-fund transactions, warehousing, GP-led secondaries.

Check:
- Minimum LPAC size and composition (typically 5 to 9 LPs, none affiliated with GP).
- Each ILPA-recommended power: explicitly granted, granted with limits, or absent.
- Quorum and voting thresholds.
- LPAC indemnification (LPAC members should be indemnified for actions in good faith).

Red flags:
- No LPAC required (RED).
- LPAC has only advisory powers, no consent rights (RED, ILPA-non-conforming).
- Affiliate transactions not subject to LPAC consent (RED).
- LPAC members not indemnified (AMBER).

Additional ILPA Model LPA expectations (often missed):
- LPAC must have the right to meet in camera (without the GP or its affiliates present). If absent: AMBER.
- LPAC must be able to appoint advisers at the expense of the Fund. If absent: AMBER.
- LPAC insurance should be permitted as a Fund expense. If absent: AMBER.
- GP must not be permitted to "pre-clear" conflicts of interest without per-event LPAC consent. If permitted: RED.
- LPA should cap the reserves the GP can hold to ensure capital is deployed. If silent or uncapped: AMBER.
- LPs must have the right to terminate or suspend the commitment period upon a sufficient vote threshold. If absent: AMBER to RED.
- Change of control of the GP or management company must require LP supermajority consent. If absent: RED.
- LPA should clarify fiduciary duty: in exercising discretion, the GP may not place its interests ahead of the Fund or LPs. If silent or contradicted: AMBER.
- Capital call notices and distribution notices should be consistent with the ILPA Capital Call and Distribution Notice Templates. If silent: AMBER.
- LPA should explicitly permit LPs to communicate with one another about fund-related issues. If absent or restricted: AMBER.
- GP should be required to furnish LPs with a list of all other LPs in the Fund on a quarterly basis. If absent: AMBER.
- Investment limits should be commitment-based, with adjustment for fund borrowing. If basis is ambiguous: AMBER.
- LPA should include "listed transactions" and "prohibited tax shelter transactions" language for US tax-exempt and pension LPs. If absent and Fund admits US tax-exempt LPs: AMBER.

## 13. Removal rights

Industry standard:
- For-cause removal (gross negligence, willful misconduct, material breach of LPA, securities law violation, bankruptcy of GP) by supermajority LP vote (typically 50%+, sometimes lower).
- Without-cause removal by supermajority (typically 75% to 90%) with reduced or zero carry to outgoing GP.
- No "two-thirds of disinterested LPs" if disinterested-vote concept is undefined.

Check:
- For-cause grounds enumerated.
- For-cause threshold (LP vote percentage).
- Without-cause: available, threshold, economic consequences.
- Severance / outgoing GP economics.
- Replacement GP mechanism.

Red flags:
- For-cause removal requires final, non-appealable court judgment (RED, slow and unworkable).
- Without-cause removal threshold above 90% (AMBER, effectively impossible).
- No without-cause removal at all (AMBER to RED, depending on for-cause workability).
- No-fault (without-cause) removal triggers payment of additional management fees to the outgoing GP (RED, ILPA-non-conforming; ILPA Model LPA explicitly removes this).
- For-cause removal does not reduce carried interest to zero (AMBER, ILPA Model LPA reduces by 100% on for-cause).
- Without-cause removal does not allow a haircut on carried interest by supermajority LP vote (AMBER).

## 14. Standard of care, exculpation, and indemnification

ILPA-recommended carveouts (always excluded from indemnification and exculpation):
- Gross negligence
- Willful misconduct
- Fraud
- Material breach of the LPA
- Violation of securities laws
- Internal fund disputes (LP-vs-GP litigation should not trigger fund indemnification of the GP)
- Tax penalties caused by GP's bad faith

Check:
- Standard of care: "ordinary care" or "good faith" (good faith is more GP-favorable; ordinary care is closer to negligence standard).
- Carveouts: each ILPA-recommended carveout present?
- Indemnification source: fund first or GP first? (ILPA: GP first, fund as backstop.)
- Advancement of expenses: with or without undertaking to repay?

Red flags:
- Indemnification covers gross negligence (RED).
- No carveout for securities law violations (RED).
- Indemnification by fund first, GP second (AMBER to RED).
- Advancement of expenses without undertaking to repay (AMBER).

## 15. Side letter mechanics

Industry standard:
- LPA expressly permits side letters.
- All side letters disclosed to all LPs (terms, not parties).
- MFN provision: LPs above a certain commitment threshold get to elect into the terms of other side letters.
- MFN election window: typically 30 to 60 days after closing.
- Scope of MFN: typically excludes regulatory carveouts (sovereign immunity, ERISA, public-disclosure terms) and economic terms granted on a quid-pro-quo basis (e.g., for first-close LPs).

Check:
- Side letters expressly permitted in the LPA?
- Disclosure obligation: all LPs receive terms (anonymised acceptable)?
- MFN threshold stated, mechanism stated, election window stated, exclusions stated and reasonable?

Red flags:
- Side letters permitted with no disclosure obligation (RED).
- No MFN provision at all (AMBER, especially if commitment-tier LPs exist).
- MFN scope excludes "any term the GP deems appropriate to exclude" (RED, illusory).
- Election window shorter than 30 days (AMBER).
