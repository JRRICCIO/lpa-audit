# LPA Audit · Acme Capital Partners SCSp · 17 May 2026

## Executive summary
Overall verdict: **Do not sign**

Top 3 findings:
1. **RED** — Open-ended SCSp offers only suspension as a liquidity management tool. AIFMD II requires at least two LMTs from the harmonised EU list with documented activation procedures.
2. **RED** — Indemnification carveout is limited to "fraud" only, with no exclusion for gross negligence, willful misconduct, material breach or securities law violations. The clause is below market and partially unenforceable under Luxembourg law.
3. **RED** — LPAC is advisory only with no consent or approval rights; combined with GP-only side-letter discretion, GP-only conflicts resolution, and a 95% + court-judgment GP removal threshold, governance is unworkable for institutional LPs.

What to fix before close: The document fails AIFMD II on liquidity management and loan origination, and falls materially below ILPA Principles 3.0 on indemnification, governance (LPAC, removal, conflicts, side letters), GP commitment and key person. It is also silent on a long list of mandatory items (AIFM identity, leverage method and cap, delegation, reporting, default, transfer, recycling, valuation, clawback). The vehicle is not signable in its current form; a full rewrite of the governance, regulatory and LP-protective architecture is required before re-circulating to LPs.

## Findings

### A. AIFMD II regulatory compliance

### 1. Leverage limits and calculation
- Clause: document is silent
- Quote: "[document is silent on leverage limits, calculation method and any LO-AIF designation]"
- Standard: AIFMD II (Directive (EU) 2024/927), Article 15 and Annex; CSSF Circular 25/901
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: Under AIFMD II, every AIF must disclose a maximum leverage level calculated under the commitment method, and any loan-originating AIF is subject to hard caps (175% open-ended, 300% closed-ended). The LPA appears to be open-ended (see 13.2) and to permit loan origination (see 20.4), which makes the silence on leverage and method a regulatory non-compliance. The omission also prevents LPs from monitoring the manager.
- Suggested redline: "The Fund's maximum leverage, calculated in accordance with the commitment method set out in Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) No 231/2013, shall not exceed 175% of net asset value. The General Partner shall report leverage quarterly to the Limited Partners and shall not exceed this cap without prior LPAC consent. The Fund qualifies as a loan-originating AIF within the meaning of AIFMD II Article 1(20a) and is subject to the leverage cap applicable to open-ended LO-AIFs."
- Owner of fix: Counsel to GP / AIFM

### 2. Loan origination
- Clause: Section 20.4
- Quote: "The Fund may originate loans to portfolio companies and third parties as the General Partner determines."
- Standard: AIFMD II Articles 15, 16, 16a; CSSF Circular 25/901
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: AIFMD II imposes (i) 5% retention on any loan transferred, (ii) prohibition on originate-to-distribute strategies, (iii) single-borrower concentration cap (20% NAV where the borrower is a financial undertaking, AIF or UCITS), (iv) annual review of credit risk, granting and pricing policies. The drafted clause grants the GP unconstrained origination discretion and pushes concentration limits to GP-set policy, with no retention, no policy review, and no governance hooks. This is a direct regulatory breach risk on day one of AIFMD II application (16 April 2026).
- Suggested redline: "The Fund may originate loans subject at all times to AIFMD II. The General Partner shall (a) retain on an ongoing basis at least 5% of the notional value of any loan transferred to a third party until maturity (or 8 years where maturity exceeds 8 years), (b) not pursue any originate-to-distribute strategy, (c) ensure that no single borrower (other than a sovereign) represents more than 20% of the Fund's capital where that borrower is a financial undertaking, AIF or UCITS, and (d) adopt, document and review at least annually a credit granting, monitoring and risk policy in accordance with CSSF Circular 25/901, which shall be made available to the LPAC on request."
- Owner of fix: Counsel to GP / AIFM

### 3. Liquidity management tools
- Clause: Section 13.2
- Quote: "the General Partner may, in its sole discretion, suspend redemptions."
- Standard: AIFMD II Article 16(2b) and Annex V (harmonised LMT list); CSSF Circular 25/901
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: For an open-ended AIF, AIFMD II requires selection of at least two LMTs from the harmonised EU list (redemption gates, swing pricing, anti-dilution levies, redemption fees, notice periods, redemption in kind, side pockets and suspension), with documented activation conditions, governance, and LP disclosure. The LPA names only suspension and grants it as a sole-discretion power with no triggers, no notice, no LPAC role and no reporting. This is non-compliant with the AIFMD II LMT regime on its face.
- Suggested redline: "The Fund adopts the following liquidity management tools in accordance with AIFMD II Annex V: (i) redemption gates, applied pro rata where aggregate redemption requests on any Redemption Date exceed 10% of the Fund's net asset value; (ii) notice periods, as set out in Section 13.2; (iii) suspension of redemptions in exceptional circumstances. The General Partner shall document the activation, calibration, governance and deactivation of each tool in a Liquidity Management Policy, shall notify the LPAC and Limited Partners of any activation within 2 business days, and shall report to the CSSF in accordance with applicable rules."
- Owner of fix: AIFM (primary) / Counsel to GP

### 4. Delegation arrangements
- Clause: document is silent
- Quote: "[document is silent on AIFM identity, delegation arrangements, and FTE / substance allocation]"
- Standard: AIFMD II Articles 7, 20; CSSF Circular 18/698 and 25/901
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: The LPA does not name the AIFM, does not describe whether portfolio or risk management is delegated, and contains no language on substance / FTE allocation. AIFMD II tightens the letter-box test and requires substantive presence in the home Member State. Without naming the AIFM and the delegation architecture, LPs cannot assess regulated counterparty risk and the GP cannot evidence non-letter-box status.
- Suggested redline: "The Fund has appointed [AIFM name], a Luxembourg authorised AIFM (CSSF register no. [•]), to perform portfolio management and risk management. Where any function is delegated, the AIFM shall (a) retain at all times the functions and senior management resources required to avoid being a letter-box entity within the meaning of AIFMD II Article 20 and CSSF Circular 18/698, (b) document delegation in a written agreement, (c) maintain a minimum of [•] full-time equivalents allocated to the Fund, and (d) make delegation arrangements available to the LPAC on request."
- Owner of fix: Counsel to GP / AIFM

### 5. Conflict of interest
- Clause: Section 15.3
- Quote: "the General Partner may resolve the matter in its sole discretion, having regard to the interests of all parties."
- Standard: AIFM Law of 12 July 2013 Article 13; AIFMD II Article 14; ILPA Principles 3.0 § Governance
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: Sole-discretion conflict resolution is not compliant with the AIFM's regulatory duty to identify, manage, mitigate and disclose conflicts and is materially below ILPA standard. LPAC consent for affiliate transactions, cross-fund trades, GP secondary involvement and warehoused investments is the market norm. The current drafting forecloses LP visibility and risks CSSF supervisory action.
- Suggested redline: "The AIFM shall maintain a written conflicts of interest policy in accordance with Article 13 of the Law of 12 July 2013 and AIFMD II. Any conflict that cannot be managed shall be disclosed to Limited Partners. The following matters shall require prior consent of the LPAC by majority: (a) any transaction between the Fund and the General Partner, the AIFM or any of their affiliates; (b) any cross-trade with another fund managed or advised by the General Partner or its affiliates; (c) any allocation of investment opportunities that departs from the Fund's allocation policy; and (d) any waiver of fees or expenses payable to an affiliate."
- Owner of fix: Counsel to GP / AIFM / LPAC

### 6. Reporting
- Clause: document is silent
- Quote: "[document is silent on Annex IV reporting, LP reporting cadence and format]"
- Standard: AIFMD II Article 24 (enhanced Annex IV); ILPA Reporting Template
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: AIFMD II expands Annex IV reporting granularity (leverage, liquidity stress, loan origination, delegation). The LPA contains no LP-facing reporting cadence. Treat silence on a mandatory regulatory and ILPA item as RED. Without an LP reporting clause, the GP can default to minimum statutory disclosure.
- Suggested redline: "The General Partner shall procure that the AIFM (a) submits Annex IV reports to the CSSF with the enhanced granularity required under AIFMD II Article 24 and (b) delivers to each Limited Partner: (i) unaudited quarterly reports within 45 days of quarter end including capital account statement, portfolio summary, leverage, liquidity profile and material events; (ii) audited annual financial statements within 120 days of year end; and (iii) an annual ILPA-template fee, expense and carried interest report."
- Owner of fix: AIFM / Counsel to GP

### 7. Sustainability
- Clause: document is silent
- Quote: "[document is silent on SFDR classification, ESG policy and PAI consideration]"
- Standard: SFDR (Regulation (EU) 2019/2088); AIFMD II read-across
- Severity: **AMBER**
- Issue: The LPA does not state an SFDR classification (Article 6 / 8 / 9), does not disclose PAI consideration, and contains no ESG investment policy hook. If LPs are EU institutionals subject to SFDR product-level disclosure, the omission creates downstream reporting friction. Severity is AMBER rather than RED because Article 6 default classification is permissible if the GP does not market sustainability features.
- Suggested redline: "The Fund is classified as a financial product within the meaning of Article [6 / 8 / 9] of Regulation (EU) 2019/2088 (SFDR). The General Partner shall make available to Limited Partners the pre-contractual, periodic and (where applicable) website disclosures required under SFDR and the Taxonomy Regulation, and shall state in the annual report whether principal adverse impacts of investment decisions on sustainability factors are considered."
- Owner of fix: Counsel to GP / AIFM

### B. ILPA Principles 3.0 market alignment

### 8. Distribution waterfall
- Clause: Section 1.1 (Preferred Return / Hurdle)
- Quote: "an amount equivalent to a return of 8% per annum, simple interest"
- Standard: ILPA Principles 3.0 § Alignment of Interests (preferred return compounding)
- Severity: **AMBER**
- Issue: 8% is market, but simple interest is below ILPA standard, which is compounding (annually, on contributed capital from date of call). Over a 10-year fund, simple vs compounding 8% is a meaningful economic transfer from LPs to GP. The LPA is also silent on waterfall structure (whole-of-fund vs deal-by-deal), catch-up, carried interest split, clawback, escrow and interim clawback — those silences are RED separately within this finding.
- Suggested redline: "'Preferred Return' means an amount equivalent to an 8% per annum return, compounded annually, calculated on contributed Capital Commitments from the date of the relevant Capital Call to the date of the relevant distribution. Distributions shall follow a whole-of-fund (European-style) waterfall: (i) return of contributed capital and fees, (ii) Preferred Return, (iii) 100% GP catch-up to 20%, (iv) 80/20 split thereafter. The General Partner shall be subject to a clawback obligation, with [25%] of distributable carried interest held in escrow until final liquidation and joint and several (or comparable creditworthy guarantee) covenant from carried interest recipients."
- Owner of fix: LP-side negotiation / Counsel to GP

### 9. Management fee
- Clause: Section 6.1
- Quote: "a Management Fee of 2.0% per annum of Aggregate Commitments during the Investment Period and 2.0% per annum of Aggregate Commitments thereafter"
- Standard: ILPA Principles 3.0 § Management Fee
- Severity: **AMBER**
- Issue: The rate of 2.0% on committed capital during the investment period is market, but ILPA standard is a step-down at the end of the investment period, typically to a lower rate applied to invested capital (net of write-downs and realisations). Continuing 2.0% on commitments through wind-down is materially LP-adverse and an outlier. The LPA is also silent on fee offsets (transaction, monitoring, break-up, directors' fees), treatment during extensions, and treatment of successor funds — those silences should also be addressed.
- Suggested redline: "From the end of the Investment Period, the Management Fee shall step down to [1.50%] per annum of the aggregate cost basis of unrealised investments, reduced by write-downs and realisations. 100% of transaction, monitoring, break-up, directors' and similar fees received by the General Partner or its affiliates from portfolio companies shall offset the Management Fee. During any extension of the Term, the Management Fee shall be further reduced to [0.75%] per annum or such lower amount as approved by the LPAC."
- Owner of fix: LP-side negotiation

### 10. GP commitment
- Clause: Section 7.2
- Quote: "in such amount as the General Partner determines, in its sole discretion ... may be funded by way of fee waiver or borrowed funds."
- Standard: ILPA Principles 3.0 § Alignment of Interests
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: A GP commitment determined unilaterally by the GP, fundable via fee waiver or borrowing, is not skin in the game and is materially below ILPA standard (typically 1-2% of commitments, in cash, not waived or financed). Fee waiver funding converts management fee economics into GP commitment, defeating alignment. Borrowed funding eliminates downside exposure. This is the single most important alignment failure in the document.
- Suggested redline: "The General Partner (or its principals) shall commit no less than 2% of Aggregate Commitments, funded in cash and not by way of management fee waiver, offset, deferral or borrowed funds. The GP Commitment shall be funded on the same terms and timing as Limited Partner commitments."
- Owner of fix: LP-side negotiation

### 11. Key person provisions
- Clause: Section 9.3
- Quote: "cease to be employed by the General Partner due to death or termination of employment ... may, at its discretion, suspend the Investment Period."
- Standard: ILPA Principles 3.0 § Key Person
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: Three structural defects. (i) Trigger covers only death and termination — no dedication-of-time prong, no incapacity, no resignation, no change-of-control of GP. (ii) Suspension is LPAC-discretionary rather than automatic. ILPA standard is automatic suspension on a key person event. (iii) No cure period, no LP vote to remediate or terminate, no successor mechanism. As drafted, the GP can keep investing after both named key persons leave if the LPAC fails to act.
- Suggested redline: "A 'Key Person Event' occurs if at any time fewer than [two] of the Key Persons (i) are alive and not incapacitated, (ii) devote substantially all of their business time to the Fund and its successor funds, or (iii) continue to be employed by, or principals of, the General Partner. Upon a Key Person Event, the Investment Period shall be automatically suspended. The General Partner shall have 180 days to propose replacement Key Persons. If at the end of the 180-day cure period, a majority in interest of the Limited Partners has not approved the proposed replacements, the Investment Period shall terminate."
- Owner of fix: LP-side negotiation / Counsel to GP

### 12. LPAC composition and powers
- Clause: Section 14.5
- Quote: "composed of representatives of three Limited Partners selected by the General Partner ... shall have no consent or approval rights"
- Standard: ILPA Principles 3.0 § Governance; CSSF supervisory practice
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: An LPAC with no consent rights, hand-picked by the GP, is governance theatre. ILPA standard expects LPAC consent for (a) affiliate transactions and conflicts, (b) valuation methodology, (c) extensions of investment period or term, (d) auditor appointment, (e) material deviations from investment policy. Composition should reflect LP economics, not GP selection. As drafted, the LPAC cannot perform any of the regulatory or governance functions other parts of the LPA assume it would (e.g. liquidity activation, conflicts consent).
- Suggested redline: "The Fund shall have an LPAC composed of representatives of the [five] largest Limited Partners by Capital Commitment, plus up to [two] additional Limited Partners appointed by a majority in interest of LPs. The LPAC shall have consent rights over: (a) any transaction between the Fund and the General Partner, AIFM or their affiliates; (b) the Fund's valuation methodology and any material change to it; (c) appointment and replacement of the auditor; (d) extension of the Investment Period or Fund Term; (e) material deviations from the Investment Policy; and (f) activation of liquidity management tools. The LPAC may meet in camera without the General Partner."
- Owner of fix: LP-side negotiation / Counsel to GP

### 13. Removal rights
- Clause: Section 19.2
- Quote: "removed only by a vote of 95% of the Limited Partners and only following a final, non-appealable court judgment of fraud or gross negligence."
- Standard: ILPA Principles 3.0 § Governance; market practice
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: 95% threshold combined with a final non-appealable court judgment makes removal effectively impossible. ILPA standard is for-cause removal at majority or 2/3 of LPs (excluding GP and affiliates) on a finding (not court judgment) of cause, plus a no-fault removal at a higher threshold (typically 75-80%) with defined economics. The LPA also fails to provide for change-of-control of GP, bankruptcy, or material breach as separate triggers.
- Suggested redline: "The General Partner may be removed (a) for Cause, by a majority in interest of the Limited Partners (excluding the General Partner and its affiliates), where 'Cause' means a finding (which may be a determination by an independent expert or arbitrator) of fraud, willful misconduct, gross negligence, material breach of this Agreement that has not been cured within 30 days, conviction for a felony or securities law violation, or bankruptcy of the General Partner; or (b) without Cause, by a vote of 75% in interest of the Limited Partners, with the economic consequences set out in Schedule [•]."
- Owner of fix: LP-side negotiation / Counsel to GP

### 14. Standard of care, exculpation, indemnification
- Clause: Section 13.4
- Quote: "indemnified ... except in cases of fraud as finally adjudicated by a court of competent jurisdiction."
- Standard: ILPA Principles 3.0 § Standard of Care; Luxembourg Civil Code Article 1382-1383 and Law of 1915; Luxembourg public policy on faute lourde
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: A fraud-only carveout is unenforceable in Luxembourg with respect to gross negligence (faute lourde) and willful misconduct (dol), which cannot be contractually excluded as a matter of public policy. The clause is also far below ILPA market: standard exclusions are gross negligence, willful misconduct, fraud, material breach, securities law violation, and bad faith. The clause should be revised both for enforceability and for market alignment.
- Suggested redline: "The General Partner, AIFM, their respective affiliates, officers, directors, employees, and agents shall be indemnified by the Fund against losses, claims, damages and expenses arising out of the performance of their duties, except to the extent such losses arise from (a) fraud, (b) willful misconduct, (c) gross negligence (faute lourde), (d) bad faith, (e) material breach of this Agreement, (f) violation of applicable securities laws, or (g) reckless disregard of duties, in each case as determined by a final judgment, arbitral award, or settlement. No advance of expenses shall be made unless the indemnified party provides an undertaking to repay if the conduct is later found to fall within the carveouts."
- Owner of fix: Counsel to GP (enforceability) / LP-side negotiation

### 15. Side letters
- Clause: Section 16.1
- Quote: "authorised to enter into side letters ... terms of side letters shall be confidential"
- Standard: ILPA Principles 3.0 § Side Letters and MFN
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: No MFN provision, no disclosure obligation, no election mechanism, no scope definition. ILPA market is for the GP to disclose all side letter terms to all LPs (subject to redactions only for legally required terms) and to offer an MFN election based on a size-tier framework. The current drafting allows the GP to grant preferential economic and governance terms invisible to other LPs.
- Suggested redline: "Within 30 days of the final closing, the General Partner shall provide to each Limited Partner a schedule of all side letter terms granted to other Limited Partners (redacted only as required by applicable law). Each Limited Partner shall have a 30-day election window to elect any side letter term granted to another Limited Partner whose Capital Commitment is equal to or less than such electing Limited Partner's Commitment, other than terms personal to the relevant Limited Partner by virtue of its regulatory, tax or organisational status."
- Owner of fix: LP-side negotiation / Counsel to GP

### C. Luxembourg SCSp / ASLP specifics

### 16. SCSp Law of 2013 compliance
- Clause: document is partly silent
- Quote: "[document is silent on GP / LP role separation, unlimited liability acknowledgment, LP register confidentiality, manager appointment]"
- Standard: Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 (as amended for SCSp); Law of 12 July 2013
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: A signable SCSp LPA must state the GP's unlimited and joint and several liability, the LPs' limited liability subject to non-management of the partnership, the confidentiality of the LP register (one of the SCSp's key features), and the appointment and removal regime of the manager (gérant). The snippet does not address these. Treat silence on mandatory SCSp items as RED.
- Suggested redline: "The General Partner is the unlimited liability partner (associé commandité) of the Fund and is jointly and severally liable for the obligations of the Fund. The Limited Partners are limited liability partners (associés commanditaires) whose liability is limited to their Capital Commitment, provided they do not perform acts of management within the meaning of Article 19 of the Law of 10 August 1915. The register of Limited Partners is confidential and shall not be made public. The General Partner shall act as manager (gérant) of the Fund."
- Owner of fix: Counsel to GP

### 17. Information rights
- Clause: document is silent
- Quote: "[document is silent on quarterly capital accounts, audited annuals, language, and AIFM data flows]"
- Standard: Law of 10 August 1915; Law of 12 July 2013; AIFMD II Article 23; ILPA Reporting Template
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: SCSp Limited Partners are entitled by law to certain information rights, and AIFMD pre-investment disclosures (Article 23) are mandatory. The LPA contains no LP information schedule. See also Finding 6 (reporting cadence). This silence is regulatory and ILPA non-compliance.
- Suggested redline: "Each Limited Partner shall be entitled to: (a) unaudited quarterly capital account statements within 45 days of quarter end; (b) audited annual financial statements within 120 days of year end, prepared in accordance with Luxembourg GAAP or IFRS; (c) annual ILPA-template fee, expense and carried interest reports; (d) the pre-investment disclosures required by Article 23 of Directive 2011/61/EU; and (e) such other information as is reasonably required to comply with applicable tax and regulatory obligations. Reports shall be in English."
- Owner of fix: Counsel to GP / AIFM

### 18. Default, transfer, recycling
- Clause: document is silent
- Quote: "[document is silent on default mechanics, transfer regime, permitted transfers, recycling cap and window]"
- Standard: Law of 10 August 1915; ILPA Principles 3.0
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: An LPA without default mechanics (loss of unfunded commitment, forfeiture, dilution, interest), transfer regime (permitted transfers, GP consent standard, ROFR), and recycling rules (cap as % of commitments, time window from realisation, treatment of return of capital vs gain) is not signable. These are mandatory commercial machinery, not optional. Treat silence as RED.
- Suggested redline: "Defaulting Limited Partner: A Limited Partner who fails to fund a Capital Call within 10 business days of notice shall be a Defaulting Partner and shall be subject to (a) interest at SOFR + 5%, (b) loss of voting rights, and (c) at the General Partner's election, forfeiture of up to 50% of its account or compelled transfer at a 25% discount. Transfers: Limited Partners may not transfer their interest without the General Partner's consent (not to be unreasonably withheld), other than to Permitted Transferees (affiliates, controlled vehicles, family entities). Recycling: The General Partner may recycle distributions representing return of capital received within 24 months of the relevant investment, up to an aggregate cap of 120% of Capital Commitments."
- Owner of fix: Counsel to GP / LP-side negotiation

### Emerging topics flagged

### E1. Valuation methodology
- Clause: document is silent
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: AIFMD II preserves the Article 19 valuation function. No valuation policy reference, no LPAC consent on methodology, and no independence safeguards are present. Material for any open-ended structure with redemptions.
- Suggested redline: "The AIFM shall adopt and disclose a written valuation policy consistent with Article 19 of Directive 2011/61/EU and AIFMD II. Material changes to the valuation methodology shall require LPAC consent. The Fund shall be valued at least quarterly by [the AIFM internally / an external valuer appointed in accordance with applicable law]."
- Owner of fix: AIFM / LPAC

### E2. Clawback (GP carried interest)
- Clause: document is silent
- Severity: **RED**
- Issue: Although the waterfall is not described, a Preferred Return / Hurdle is defined, which implies carried interest. Silence on clawback, escrow, interim clawback test and personal recourse / guarantee is materially LP-adverse and below ILPA standard. See Finding 8 for redline.
- Owner of fix: LP-side negotiation

### E3. Change of control of GP
- Clause: document is silent
- Severity: **AMBER**
- Issue: No LPAC or LP consent right on change of control of the GP or AIFM. Market practice is to require LPAC consent or to trigger a key person / for-cause removal.
- Suggested redline: "Any direct or indirect change of control of the General Partner or the AIFM shall require the prior consent of the LPAC. Failure to obtain such consent shall constitute Cause for removal of the General Partner under Section 19.2."
- Owner of fix: LP-side negotiation

## Scope statement
This audit covered: the 18 areas listed in the prompt (AIFMD II compliance — leverage, loan origination, liquidity management, delegation, conflicts, reporting, sustainability; ILPA Principles 3.0 — waterfall, management fee, GP commitment, key person, LPAC, removal, indemnification, side letters; Luxembourg SCSp specifics — Law of 2013 compliance, information rights, default/transfer/recycling), plus three emerging topics surfaced by the document (valuation, clawback, change of control of GP).
This audit did not cover: US securities law (Investment Advisers Act, Section 3(c)(1)/(7)), ERISA / VCOC analysis, Luxembourg tax structuring (subscription tax, MLI, ATAD III, Pillar 2), AML operational procedures and KYC programme, EU pre-marketing rules (AIFMD Article 30a), ELTIF 2.0 alignment.
Specialist follow-up recommended on: (i) Luxembourg counsel sign-off on the indemnification carveouts and on the SCSp manager appointment language; (ii) AIFM substance and delegation memo, in light of CSSF Circular 18/698 and AIFMD II Article 20; (iii) tax counsel review of GP fee waiver mechanics if retained in any form.

## Three questions for senior review
1. Given an open-ended structure that originates loans, has only suspension as an LMT, and is silent on leverage and concentration, do we treat this as an AIFMD II compliance defect to fix before close, or do we walk away from the mandate?
2. The indemnification carveout is limited to fraud — under Luxembourg public policy faute lourde and dol cannot be contractually excluded. Should we surface this with the GP's counsel as an enforceability point (which is harder to refuse) rather than only as an ILPA market argument?
3. The LPAC is advisory only, the GP picks its members, removal is effectively impossible, and side letters are confidential — does the LP we are advising have the leverage to fix the governance package, or is the strategic call to decline given that the governance defects compound?
